# Candidate Positioning Matrix — Why a Cross-Domain AR Evidence Review Is Still Needed

**Use:** Introduction / prior-review positioning after NEXT-05 freeze. Secondary evidence only.

| Review | Domain | Included evidence | Limitation relevant to this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| Li et al. 2026 | spatial neglect rehabilitation/assessment | 15 studies; 567 participants | small samples, methodological heterogeneity, limited RCTs; low-to-very-low certainty |
| El Ashry et al. 2026 | surgical training | 11 studies; 347 participants | heterogeneous hardware/outcomes; studies do not align cleanly; long-term retention/cost evidence limited |
| Man et al. 2026 | navigation | systematic review + meta-analysis | domain-specific synthesis; does not provide field-wide algorithm/device/reproducibility map |
| Kostakopoulos et al. 2026 | robotic urology | 38 studies | low-quality/high-risk evidence motivates qualitative synthesis and further prospective RCTs |
| Xu et al. 2026 | dental implant placement | 21 studies | clinical/technical factors materially influence accuracy; domain-specific evidence |

## Positioning claim
Recent reviews are valuable but predominantly **domain- or outcome-specific**. The proposed TVCG review addresses a different question: how AR evidence is structured across technologies, algorithms, benchmarks, devices/sensors, environments, users, metrics and reproducibility, and where comparisons are or are not scientifically valid.

This matrix must not be used to claim that no prior broad AR review exists. Broad/umbrella reviews already in the review corpus must be discussed explicitly.
