# Candidate Result — Industrial AR Performance/Workload Contradiction Cluster

**Status: pre-freeze candidate; do not report as field prevalence yet.**

Validated primary studies already show that the direction of AR's effect is conditional rather than uniformly beneficial.

| Study | Context | Comparator | Performance signal | Workload/human-factor signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drouot et al. 2022 | real industrial assembly line | computerized instructions | AR increased assembly time; no error reduction | eye/behavioral measures indicated higher workload for simple workstation |
| Yang et al. 2019 | staged assembly experiments | non-AR assistance conditions | AR shortened several task/subtask times and reduced mistakes | workload effect varied by commissioning/joining stage and complexity |
| Atici-Ulusu et al. 2021 | automotive assembly | current work procedure | task context comparison | NASA-TLX lower with AR glasses; EEG also measured |
| Seeliger et al. 2023 | industrial quality inspection | screen / no guidance | HMD AR improved task performance; benefit moderated by difficulty | lower mental workload and positive UX |
| Marino et al. 2024 | assembly guidance | technical documents | tablet AR improved user performance, especially for unfamiliar tasks | cognitive workload evaluated |

## Candidate interpretation
The emerging explanation is not simply “AR works” versus “AR does not work.” Effects appear contingent on **task stage, task difficulty, device/interface, comparator and outcome definition**. This is exactly the kind of contradiction that the Evidence Cube and C0–C3 comparability framework are intended to resolve.

## Paper use
If this pattern survives the frozen systematic corpus, it can become a Results subsection and a Discussion argument against universal AR-effect claims.
