# Candidate Accessibility and Inclusive-AR Evidence Model

Represent accessibility evidence as:

A = (AccessNeed, InterfaceDemand, Adaptation, Context, Evaluation, Outcome, Participation)

## Access-need dimensions
- visual: acuity, field loss, contrast/color, depth/perception;
- auditory: speech/audio cue access;
- motor: reach, dexterity, tremor, mobility, controller/gesture demands;
- cognitive/neurodiverse: attention, executive demand, sensory sensitivity, communication/social support;
- age-related: combinations of sensory, motor, cognitive and technology-familiarity demands;
- multiple/intersectional needs.

## Interface-demand dimensions
visual density/field placement; gesture precision; gaze demand; speech demand; controller/touch demand; locomotion; calibration/setup; device weight/comfort; cognitive/workload demand.

## Adaptation dimensions
magnification/remapping; contrast/visual enhancement; alternative modality; simplified interaction; adaptive pacing/content; personalization; assistive navigation; caregiver/teacher support.

## Evaluation rule
Population membership alone is not accessibility evidence. Code:
1. the access barrier or need;
2. the interface demand;
3. the adaptation;
4. whether disabled/target users participated;
5. accessibility-specific outcome/metric;
6. field duration and context.

## Evidence basis
- Pur et al. 2023: low-vision AR/VR review focused on visual field expansion and visual acuity.
- Berenguer et al. 2020: autism-specific AR review in children/adolescents.
- Baragash et al. 2022: 57 older-adult VR/AR studies mapped across physical, cognitive, psychological and social well-being.
- Ceyssens et al. 2025: AR-specific older-adult well-being systematic review.
- 2026 older-adult scoping review: 41 studies and practical headset/control/calibration accessibility concerns; retained as supplementary discovery until final corpus.
- Shidende et al. 2023 accessibility-evaluation review: relevant discovery lead, withheld from master.bib pending complete metadata verification.

## Candidate gap test
Accessibility is considered structurally weak only if final-corpus evidence shows sparse target-user participation, weak barrier-specific evaluation, or persistent access-need × interface-demand cells—not simply because a paper does not use the word accessibility.
