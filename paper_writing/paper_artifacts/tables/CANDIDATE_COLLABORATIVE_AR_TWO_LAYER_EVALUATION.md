# Candidate Result — Collaborative AR Needs Separate Social and Performance Outcomes

**Pre-freeze synthesis.**

The collaborative-AR evidence increasingly shows that subjective/social improvements and task-time improvements need not coincide.

- Marques et al. argue that collaborative AR evaluation must move beyond supporting-technology performance toward collaboration-specific dimensions.
- Yang et al. show how users physically and conversationally coordinate around a shared collocated AR artifact, while also reporting interaction breakdowns/fatigue.
- Rasch et al. (N=48) find that shared gaze can increase shared attention and improve perceived collaborative experience while having limited effect on completion time for the studied tasks.
- CroCoDL shows that collaborative/shared AR also has a lower-level systems prerequisite: heterogeneous devices must localize consistently into a shared reference.

## Candidate implication
Collaborative AR should be evaluated on at least two coupled layers:
1. **shared-system layer:** cross-device registration/localization, consistency, latency;
2. **human-collaboration layer:** shared attention, coordination, social connectedness, workload, task outcome.

A single completion-time metric is therefore structurally insufficient.
