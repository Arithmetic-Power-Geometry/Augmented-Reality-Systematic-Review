# Candidate Result — Accessibility Requires Multi-Dimensional Evaluation

**Pre-freeze synthesis; not a final prevalence claim.**

| Study | Accessibility task | Benefit signal | Limitation/tradeoff signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coughlan & Miele 2017 | AR tools for low vision/blindness | establishes AR4VI design space | conceptual/system examples rather than one universal effectiveness estimate |
| Huang et al. 2019 | hallway sign reading/navigation | more direct walking routes; users found assistance helpful | AR users walked more slowly and took longer |
| Gopalakrishnan et al. 2020 | visual acuity support | prospective evidence across 100 low-vision patients | heterogeneous visual-loss categories require stratification |
| Lang & Machulla 2021 | touchscreen interaction | augmentations improved interaction and reduced cognitive load, especially unfamiliar layouts | benefit depends on visual design/task |
| Salameh et al. 2026 | museum navigation/exhibit access | mobility/visibility support and stronger sense of security | accuracy and responsiveness challenges |

## Candidate implication
Accessibility evaluation should not collapse to a binary “AR helps/does not help” outcome. At minimum, the Evidence Cube should retain **impairment/user group × task × augmentation modality × performance × time × workload × usability/safety**.

This is a candidate synthesis to be tested after full corpus freeze.
