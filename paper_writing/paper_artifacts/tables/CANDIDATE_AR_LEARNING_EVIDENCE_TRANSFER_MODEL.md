# Candidate AR Learning-Evidence and Transfer Model

Represent an educational AR intervention as:
L = (Learner, Domain, Pedagogy, ARFunction, Exposure, Comparator, Outcome, TimeHorizon, Transfer, CognitiveLoad, Context)

## Outcome classes
L1 immediate knowledge/achievement;
L2 conceptual understanding;
L3 procedural/psychomotor skill;
L4 retention/delayed recall;
L5 near transfer;
L6 far transfer/application;
L7 motivation/interest/engagement;
L8 cognitive load/workload;
L9 collaboration/social learning;
L10 self-efficacy/acceptance;
L11 classroom/teacher adoption and implementation burden.

## Evidence horizon
E0 single-session reaction/usability;
E1 immediate post-test;
E2 repeated intervention;
E3 delayed retention;
E4 transfer to new task/problem;
E5 authentic classroom/workplace performance;
E6 longitudinal adoption or sustained learning.

## Moderators
learner age/prior knowledge; subject; pedagogy; scaffolding; collaboration; device; AR registration mode; feedback; intervention frequency/duration; teacher assistance; novelty; comparator quality.

## Prior evidence
- Garzón & Acevedo 2019: 64 quantitative papers; medium overall learning-gain effect.
- Garzón et al. 2020: 41 quantitative studies; pedagogical approach moderates AR educational impact.
- Zhang et al. 2022: 129 K-12 articles; large pooled effect with significant moderators.
- Zhu et al. 2026: 27 studies; cognitive-load effect g=-0.383 with I²=92.7%, showing strong heterogeneity.
- Huang et al. 2025: 124 MR-education papers, 32 in meta-analysis; course duration, device-use frequency and teacher help matter.
- Gonnermann-Müller & Krüger 2025 already in master.bib: design-specific cognitive-load recommendations.

## Core synthesis rules
1. Immediate achievement != retention != transfer.
2. Engagement/motivation != learning gain.
3. Reduced cognitive load is not always equivalent to better learning; load type and task must be considered.
4. AR-medium comparisons do not identify which AR design mechanism caused an effect.
5. Pooled effects must preserve moderators and heterogeneity.
6. A short laboratory intervention cannot establish sustained classroom adoption.
