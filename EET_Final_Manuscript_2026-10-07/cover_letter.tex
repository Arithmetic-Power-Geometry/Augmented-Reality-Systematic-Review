\begin{letter}{Editor\\IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics}
\opening{Dear Editor,}
Please consider the manuscript ``Evidence Entitlement Theory for Augmented Reality Research: From Heterogeneous Evidence to Licensed Scientific Claims.'' The manuscript introduces EET as an integration-level evidence-synthesis framework that maps heterogeneous evidence to explicitly scoped scientific claims, maximal entitlement frontiers, blocking obligations, transfer boundaries, and perturbation diagnostics.

The study combines a frozen 200-world development benchmark, prespecified stress and ablation analyses, a fresh 200-world confirmatory benchmark, and retrospective application to published AR evidence. The manuscript does not claim that EET replaces meta-analysis, does not claim firstness for its mathematical primitives, and does not treat published evidence as a real-world ground-truth oracle.

No new human-participant, survey, interview, laboratory, field, clinical, or physical AR experiment was conducted. Data, code, benchmark specifications, and committed CSV artifacts are publicly available in the project repository.

All reported quantitative claims are traceable to frozen repository artifacts.
\closing{Sincerely,}
